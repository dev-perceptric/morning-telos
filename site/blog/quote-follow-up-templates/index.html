25+ Quote Follow-Up Text & Email Templates to Win More Jobs

Copy-and-paste quote follow-up texts and emails for every stage, from the day you send the quote to the final check-in, plus what to send after you win or lose.

Short answer: A good quote follow-up is short, friendly and goes out on a schedule: the same day you send the quote, 2 days later, about a week later, and one final check-in. For example: "Hi [Name], just making sure the quote for [Service] came through OK. Any questions I can answer?" Or: "Are you still interested in [Service]? Reply 1 for yes, 2 for not right now."

This guide is for contractors, home service pros, insurance agents and anyone else who sends quotes for a living.

Plenty of quotes end in silence. The customer got busy, forgot, or is waiting for a nudge. A polite follow-up at the right time is often all it takes to win the job.

Below you'll find 33 quote follow-up templates, grouped by when to send them. Copy them, swap in your details, and send.

What makes a good quote follow-up?

  • Send the first one the same day. The visit and the price are still fresh in the customer's mind.
  • Keep texts short. One or two sentences is plenty. Make the next step obvious.
  • Make it easy to reply. Ask a yes or no question, or give them a number or word to reply with.
  • Add something useful each time. Answer a common question or offer another option. "Just checking in" gets old after the first try.
  • Use your own name. People say yes to a person they've met, so sign texts and emails as yourself.
  • Know when to stop. After three or four tries, send a polite last message and close the file.

33 quote follow-up templates

Each group below matches a step in a simple follow-up schedule. Replace anything in [brackets] with your own details.

Same day: when you send the quote

Text right after you send the quote

Hi [Name], it's [Staff name] from [Business]. I just emailed your quote for [Service]. Take a look when you have a minute, and text me here with any questions. Happy to walk you through it.

Email with the quote attached

Subject: Your [Service] quote from [Business] Hi [Name], Thanks for having us out today. Your quote for [Service] is attached, and you can also view it online here: [Link] Quick summary: - What's included: [short list of the work] - Price: [Amount] - Earliest start date: [Date] If anything is unclear or you'd like to change the scope, just reply to this email or call me at [Phone]. I'm glad to adjust it. Thanks, [Staff name] [Business]

Text after an in-person estimate

Hi [Name], thanks for showing me around today. I'll have your written quote for [Service] to you by [Time] tomorrow. If you think of anything else you'd like included, just text it here.

Text when the quote was requested online

Hi [Name], this is [Business]. Thanks for your quote request for [Service]. Your estimate is ready here: [Link]. Want to talk it over? Reply with a good time and I'll give you a call.

Two days later: a friendly check-in

Friendly check-in text

Hi [Name], it's [Staff name] at [Business]. Just making sure the quote for [Service] came through OK. Any questions I can answer?

Email that answers common questions

Subject: A few answers about your [Service] quote Hi [Name], I wanted to follow up on the quote I sent on [Day]. Most people ask us the same few things at this point, so here are quick answers: - How long will it take? About [number] days. - Do you clean up after? Yes, we leave the site clean at the end of every day. - Is the work guaranteed? Yes, [guarantee details]. - When can you start? Our next opening is [Date]. If you have a question I didn't cover, just reply here. Thanks, [Staff name] [Business] [Phone]

Text with an easy next step

Hi [Name], [Staff name] from [Business] here. If the quote looks good, you can approve it right here: [Link]. Then I'll get you on the schedule. No rush, I just wanted to make it easy.

One week later: are they still interested?

One week check-in text

Hi [Name], it's [Staff name] with [Business]. It's been about a week since I sent your [Service] quote. Still thinking it over? I'm happy to answer questions or adjust the plan. Reply STOP to opt out.

Still interested? (quick reply text)

Hi [Name], quick question from [Business]: are you still interested in [Service]? Reply 1 for yes, 2 for not right now, or 3 if you have questions. Reply STOP to opt out.

One week email

If you're still deciding, I'm happy to: - Walk through the quote with you on a quick call - Adjust the scope to fit your budget - Hold a spot on our schedule for [Date] Reply here or call me at [Phone]. Best, [Staff name] [Business]

The final follow-up

Last check-in text

Hi [Name], this is my last check-in about your [Service] quote. If now isn't the right time, no problem at all. If you want to move ahead later, just text this number and we'll pick up where we left off.

Final follow-up email

I don't want to fill up your inbox, so this will be my last email about it. If you're still interested, just reply "yes" and I'll get things moving. If you went with someone else or decided to wait, I understand. I'll keep your quote on file until [Date] in case you change your mind. Thanks for considering us, [Staff name] [Business]

Final text with a one-word reply

Hi [Name], [Business] here. Should I keep your [Service] quote open or close it out for now? Just reply OPEN or CLOSE. Either answer is totally fine.

When to send quote follow-ups

Here's a simple schedule that works for most service businesses. Stop the follow-ups as soon as the customer replies.

When What to send Best channel
Same day The quote, plus a short text so they know it's there Email and text
Day 2 A friendly check-in or answers to common questions Text
Day 7 "Still interested?" with an easy reply Text or email
Day 10 to 14 Options, a payment plan or a seasonal reason to book Email
Day 21 to 30 A polite final follow-up Text and email
After they decide Next steps if you win, thanks and a feedback ask if you lose Text or email

For bigger jobs, like a new roof or a remodel, you can space these out a bit more. Customers often need time to talk it over at home.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Waiting too long. If your first follow-up goes out a week later, the customer may have already hired someone else.
  • Sending "just checking in" every time. Give a reason for each message, like an answer, a photo or an opening on your schedule.
  • Writing long texts. Save the details for email.
  • Cutting your price right away. Ask what's driving the concern first. Often a smaller scope or a payment plan solves it.
  • Losing track of open quotes. Keep every quote in a CRM or at least a shared spreadsheet with the next follow-up date.
  • Texting at odd hours. Send texts during business hours, and always honor STOP requests.

FAQ

How soon should you follow up on a quote?

Send a short text the same day you send the quote, or the next morning at the latest. Then check in again about 2 days later and once more after a week.

How many times should you follow up on a quote?

Three to five follow-ups over two to four weeks is a good range. Each one should add something new, like an answer, an option or a reason to book now. End with a polite final message so the customer can give you a clear yes or no.

Should I follow up on a quote by text or email?

Use both. Texts work well for quick check-ins and yes or no questions. Email is better for details, like options, prices and next steps.

What do you say when a customer says your quote is too high?

Thank them and ask what number they had in mind. Then offer ways to lower the price, like a smaller scope, different materials or a payment plan. Ask questions before you cut your price.

Is it OK to text customers about a quote?

Yes, if they gave you their number and agreed to hear from you by text. Send texts during business hours, include your business name, and let people reply STOP to opt out of follow-ups.
