25+ Appointment Reminder Email Templates (Copy & Paste)

Free appointment reminder email templates with subject lines: 1 week before, the day before, prep steps, video calls, rescheduling, no-shows and first visits.

Short answer: A good appointment reminder email has a subject line with the date, the time and place, and a one-click way to confirm or reschedule. For example, subject "See you tomorrow at [Time]," then: "Hi [Name], just a reminder that your [Service] is tomorrow, [Date], at [Time]. Need to reschedule? [Link]." Send one a week before and one the day before.

Email is the best place for the details: directions, parking, forms and what to bring. A clear reminder email means fewer no-shows and fewer "where do I park?" calls.

This guide is for clinics, law firms, accounting offices and any business that books appointments. Below are 32 copy-and-paste reminder emails with subject lines, grouped by when and why you send them.

What makes a good appointment reminder email?

  • Put the date in the subject line: Clients should know what the email is about from their inbox.
  • Lead with the key facts: Day, date, time and place go near the top, on their own lines.
  • Make the next step one click: A confirm or reschedule link beats "call us during office hours."
  • Add prep notes only when needed: Forms, documents or parking info help. Extra fluff gets skipped.
  • Use a real reply-to address: Clients will hit reply. Make sure someone reads it.
  • Keep private details out: Skip health conditions, case details and account numbers.

1 week before

Standard 1-week reminder

Subject: Your appointment on [Date] Hi [Name], This is a friendly reminder that your [Service] with [Staff name] is coming up next week. Date: [Day], [Date] Time: [Time] Where: [Address] If you need to change your time, you can reschedule here: [Link] See you soon, [Business] [Phone]

With confirm button

Subject: Please confirm your appointment on [Date] Hi [Name], You're booked for [Service] on [Day], [Date] at [Time]. Please take a second to confirm so we can hold your spot. Confirm here: [Link] Can't make it? Use the same link to pick a new time. Thanks, [Staff name] [Business]

Booked months ago

Subject: Still good for [Date]? Hi [Name], You booked this appointment a while back, so we wanted to check in. You're scheduled for [Service] on [Day], [Date] at [Time]. If that still works, no need to do anything. If not, reply to this email or call us at [Phone] and we'll find a better time. Talk soon, [Business]

With add-to-calendar

Subject: Add your [Service] to your calendar Hi [Name], Your appointment at [Business] is one week away: [Day], [Date] at [Time]. Add it to your calendar so you don't miss it: [Link] We're at [Address]. Parking is available [parking details]. See you then, [Business]

Day-before reminders

Mistakes to avoid

  • Vague subject lines: "Reminder" alone gets lost. Add the date or time.
  • Hiding the time: Don't bury the appointment details under a long greeting.
  • No way to reschedule: If the only option is calling, some people skip the visit instead.
  • Sending from a no-reply address: Clients will reply with questions. Make sure those replies reach someone.
  • Too many emails: Two reminders plus prep notes is usually enough.
  • Wrong time zone: For video calls, state the time zone, especially if clients live in other states.

FAQ

What should an appointment reminder email include?

Use a clear subject line with the date. In the email, include the client's name, the day, date and time, the address or video link, and an easy way to confirm or reschedule. Add prep notes only if the visit needs them.

When should you send an appointment reminder email?

Send one about a week before and one the day before. For visits booked far ahead, the week-before email matters most. Pair email with a same-day text for the best coverage.

What is a good subject line for an appointment reminder email?

Keep it short and put the date or time in it, like "See you tomorrow at [Time]" or "Your appointment on [Date]." Clients should know what the email is about without opening it.

Should I use email or text for appointment reminders?

Use both. Email is better for details like directions, forms and prep steps. Text is better for quick reminders close to the appointment.

Can I send appointment reminder emails automatically?

Yes. Most booking and practice management tools can send reminder emails on a schedule. For custom prep notes, follow-ups after no-shows or syncing with other tools, an automation can fill the gaps.
