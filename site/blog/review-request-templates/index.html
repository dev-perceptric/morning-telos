30+ Review Request Text & Email Templates (Get More Google Reviews)

Copy-and-paste review request texts, emails and in-person scripts, plus replies for 5-star and negative Google reviews.

Short answer: Ask within a few hours of the visit, keep it short and send a direct link. For example: "Hi [Name], thanks for choosing [Business] today! Would you leave us a quick Google review? [Link]"

Reviews are often the first thing a new customer checks before they call. Many happy customers will leave one if you ask at the right time and make it easy.

This guide is for owners and managers of local businesses who want more Google reviews without being pushy.

Inside you'll find 40 review request templates. They include texts, emails, in-person scripts and replies for both 5-star and negative reviews.

What makes a good review request?

  • Ask soon: the best time is within a few hours of the job, while the experience is fresh.
  • Make it one tap: use your direct Google review link so customers land right on the form.
  • Keep it short: one or two sentences for a text. Customers decide in seconds.
  • Make it personal: use their name and, when you can, the name of the person who helped them.
  • Ask everyone the same way: neutral words like "how did we do?" work for every customer.
  • Ask twice at most: one request and one reminder is plenty.

Replying to a negative review

Stay calm, apologize and move the conversation to a phone call. Keep private details out of public replies.

Apologize and take it offline

Tips and mistakes to avoid

  • Don't offer rewards for reviews. Google's policies don't allow discounts, gifts or other incentives in exchange for a review.
  • Don't ask only happy customers. Picking and choosing who gets asked is called review gating, and Google's policies discourage it. Send the same request to everyone.
  • Use a direct link. It takes customers straight to the review form, with no searching.
  • Don't ask for 5 stars. Asking for a set rating feels pushy. Ask for honest feedback instead.
  • Reply to every review. A short thank you is enough for good ones. For bad ones, apologize and offer a call.
  • Protect privacy. Clinics, law firms and other offices should never confirm someone is a client in a public reply.
  • Get permission for texts. Only text customers who gave you their number for that purpose, and include a way to opt out.

FAQ

When is the best time to ask for a review?

Within a few hours of the job or visit, while it's fresh. A text works best for the first ask. A next-day email and one reminder a few days later can catch people who missed it.

Can I offer a discount for a Google review?

No. Google's policies don't allow offering discounts, gifts or other rewards in exchange for reviews. Ask nicely and make it easy instead.

Should I only ask happy customers for reviews?

No. Asking only customers you expect to be happy is called review gating, and Google's policies discourage it. Send the same request to every customer.

How do I get my Google review link?

Sign in to your Google Business Profile and look for the option to ask for reviews or share a review form. Copy that link and use it wherever a template says [Link].

How many times should I ask for a review?

Twice is usually enough: one request and one reminder. More than that can annoy customers.
