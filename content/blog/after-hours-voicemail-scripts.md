> **Short answer:** A good after-hours voicemail greeting says your business name, tells callers you're closed, gives your hours and says when you'll call back. If you offer emergency service, explain how to reach the on-call line, and tell anyone with a life-threatening emergency to call 911. Example: "Hi, you've reached [Business]. We're closed right now. Leave your name and number and we'll call you back tomorrow by [Time]."

Calls don't stop when you lock the door. People call about a leaky pipe at 8 p.m., a sick child on Saturday or a legal problem over a holiday weekend.

## What makes a good after-hours voicemail greeting?

- **Say your business name first.** Callers want to know right away that they dialed the right number.
- **Give your hours.** Tell people when you open again so they know when to expect a call.
- **Say when you'll call back.** "By 10 a.m. tomorrow" is more reassuring than "as soon as possible."
- **Handle emergencies clearly.** Tell callers with a life-threatening emergency to hang up and call 911. Give urgent callers your on-call number or a menu option.
- **Keep it short.** Aim for 20 to 30 seconds. Put the most important details at the start.
- **Give another way to reach you.** A booking link or "text this number" helps callers who won't leave a message.

## Voicemail, answering service or AI receptionist?

A voicemail greeting is free and takes five minutes to record. The trade-off is that every caller has to wait for a callback, and some will call someone else instead.

Having a person answer costs more. A full-time receptionist earns a median of $38,010 a year, or $18.27 an hour, according to the [U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/office-and-administrative-support/receptionists.htm). One person also can't cover every night and weekend.

## Tips and mistakes to avoid

- **Forgetting to change your holiday greeting back.** Set a calendar reminder for the day you reopen.
- **Promising a callback time you can't keep.** If you say "by 10 a.m.," make sure someone actually calls by then.
- **Rambling.** Long greetings make people hang up before the beep.
- **Leaving out the 911 line.** If you run any kind of emergency line, always tell callers with a life-threatening emergency to call 911 first.
- **Asking for private details.** Clinics and law firms shouldn't ask callers to leave medical or case details by text.
- **Letting your mailbox fill up.** A full mailbox means callers can't leave a message at all. Clear it every morning.
