> **Short answer:** A good dental appointment reminder gives the office name, the day and time, and an easy way to confirm or reschedule. Send one 2 to 3 days before the visit and one the day before. For example: "Hi [Name], this is [Business]. Reminder: your cleaning is [Day], [Date] at [Time]. Reply C to confirm or R to reschedule."

This guide is for dental offices that want fewer empty chairs and less time on the phone. Every template below is ready to copy, paste and send.

## What makes a good dental appointment reminder?

- **Lead with your office name:** Patients may not have your number saved. Put [Business] in the first line so they know who's texting.
- **Give the day, date and time:** Write "Tuesday, March 4 at 9:30 AM." The day of the week helps people spot mistakes.
- **Keep health details out:** Name the appointment type, like "cleaning" or "follow-up visit," and nothing more.
- **Make confirming easy:** Ask for a one-letter reply, like C to confirm or R to reschedule.
- **Only add what matters:** Mention forms, insurance cards or parking only when the patient needs them for this visit.
- **Use the channel they prefer:** Short reminders work well as texts. Save email for longer details like forms and first-visit info.

## When to send dental reminders

| Message | When to send | Channel |
|---|---|---|
| New patient welcome | Right after booking | Text and email |
| Forms and insurance | 3 to 7 days before | Email, then a text |
| First reminder | 2 to 3 days before | Text |
| Day-before reminder | 1 day before | Text or email |
| Same-day reminder | About 2 hours before | Text |
| No reply to confirm | Afternoon of the day before | Text, then a call |
| Missed appointment | Same day, then 2 to 3 days later | Text, then email |
| Cleaning recall | 2 to 4 weeks before they're due | Text and email |
| Overdue recall | Every 1 to 2 months, up to 3 or 4 tries | Text and email |

## Tips and mistakes to avoid

- **Keep health details out of every message.** Texts can pop up on a locked screen or land on a shared family phone. Name the appointment type and stop there. Skip diagnoses, tooth numbers, treatment notes and balances. If you're in the U.S., ask your compliance lead how HIPAA applies to your reminders.
- **Get consent first.** Ask patients at intake if they want texts, and note it in their file.
- **Don't over-send.** Two or three reminders per visit is plenty. More than that starts to feel like nagging.
- **Send during normal hours.** Aim for about 8 AM to 8 PM in the patient's time zone.
- **Text from a number that takes replies.** If a patient replies R and nobody sees it, you've lost the visit anyway.
- **Honor STOP right away.** Add "Reply STOP to opt out" to recall and follow-up texts.
- **Check your templates every few months.** Update them when your hours, address or policies change.
