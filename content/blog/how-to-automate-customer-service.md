## What can you automate in customer service?

Customer service automation means software handles the routine parts of support so your team can focus on the hard ones. Here is what most businesses can automate today:

- **Common questions:** hours, pricing, shipping times, return policy, how-to steps.
- **Order and account lookups:** "Where is my order?", "When is my next appointment?", "What plan am I on?"
- **Simple actions:** rebooking, address changes, cancellations within your rules, sending a return label.
- **Ticket sorting:** tagging each ticket by topic and urgency, then routing it to the right person.
- **Replies your team approves:** AI drafts the answer and a person clicks send.
- **After-hours coverage:** answering chat, email and even phone calls at night and on weekends.

## How to automate customer service in 7 steps

### 1. Pull a month of conversations

Export your last 30 days of emails, chats and tickets. If you don't have a help desk yet, start with your inbox and phone log.

### 2. Group them by topic

Sort every conversation into buckets like "order status", "returns", "pricing", "booking" and "technical issue". Count each bucket. In most businesses the top five buckets cover the majority of volume.

### 3. Pick the safest, most common topic first

Choose a topic with a clear right answer and low risk, like order status or opening hours. Save complaints and refunds for later.

### 4. Write the source of truth

Write short, plain answers for each question in that topic, plus your rules ("Refunds under $50 are approved automatically"). This becomes the knowledge your AI uses. Bad source material is the number one reason AI support gives wrong answers.

### 5. Connect AI to your tools

Connect an AI assistant to your help desk and to the systems that hold the answers, like Shopify for orders or your booking tool for appointments. Without that connection, AI can only repeat your FAQ. With it, AI can look things up and take action.

### 6. Set the handoff rules

Decide exactly when a person takes over: an angry customer, a refund above your limit, a question the AI isn't sure about, or any time the customer asks. Pass the full conversation along so nobody has to ask twice.

### 7. Review every week and expand

Read a sample of AI conversations every week. Fix wrong answers in your source of truth, then add the next topic. Track three numbers: share of tickets resolved without a person, first response time and customer satisfaction.

## Customer service automation tools

| Tool type | Examples | Best for | Typical cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Help desk with built-in AI | Zendesk AI, Intercom Fin, Gorgias AI, Help Scout AI | Teams that already use that help desk | Usually priced per AI resolution or per seat |
| AI chat for websites | Tidio, Chatbase, Crisp | Small sites that need an AI chat widget fast | Monthly plans, often with a free tier |
| AI receptionist (phone) | Smith.ai, Goodcall, Rosie, Upfirst | Businesses that get a lot of calls | About $25 to $500 a month ([full price breakdown](/blog/ai-receptionist-cost/)) |
| Automation platforms | Zapier, Make, n8n | Connecting tools and moving data between them | Free tiers, then monthly plans by usage |
| Custom AI support agent | Built by a developer or agency | Unique rules, many systems, high volume | $5,000 to $25,000 to build, plus a monthly fee |

Prices change often, so check each vendor's pricing page before you buy.

## When should you get help?

Off-the-shelf tools are a great place to start. Bring in help when:

- The AI needs data from several systems, like your store, CRM and shipping tool.
- Your rules are unusual, like custom refund logic or industry requirements.
- You bought a tool but never finished setting it up.
- Volume is high enough that per-resolution fees add up to more than a custom build.
