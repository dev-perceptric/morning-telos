> **Short answer:** A good appointment reminder text has your business name, the date and time, and one easy action. For example: "Hi [Name], this is [Business]. You're booked for [Service] on [Date] at [Time]. Reply YES to confirm or R to reschedule." Send one 2 to 3 days before, one the day before, and one a couple of hours before.

No-shows hurt. An empty slot is lost money, and it's a spot another client could have used. A short text at the right time fixes most of it.

This guide is for clinics, salons, gyms, home service companies and anyone else who books appointments. Below you'll find 52 copy-and-paste reminder texts, grouped by when you send them, plus versions for specific industries.

## What makes a good appointment reminder text?

- **Say who you are first:** Start with your business name so the client knows the text is real.
- **Give the exact day, date and time:** "Tomorrow" alone is easy to misread. Add the date.
- **Ask for one action:** "Reply YES to confirm or R to reschedule" is easy to answer in a second.
- **Keep it short:** Aim for under 160 characters when you can. Long texts get split or skipped.
- **Make changes easy:** A link or a one-letter reply beats "call during business hours."
- **Protect privacy:** Leave out health details, case details and anything personal.

## When to send appointment reminder texts

| When | What to send | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Right after booking | Booking confirmation with date, time and address | Proves the booking went through |
| 2 to 3 days before | First reminder with a confirm request | Gives time to reschedule and refill the slot |
| 1 day before | Day-before reminder | Catches people who forgot to reply |
| 1 to 2 hours before | Same-day reminder with address or link | Helps with directions and timing |
| After the visit | Thank you or next booking | Brings clients back |

For visits booked weeks or months out, add one more reminder about a week before.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Texting too often:** Two or three reminders per visit is plenty. More feels like spam.
- **Sending at odd hours:** Stick to roughly 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. in the client's time zone.
- **Using a no-reply number:** Clients will text back. Make sure someone, or something, reads the replies.
- **Forgetting the address:** First-time clients need it. Add a map link for same-day texts.
- **Adding too much:** One appointment, one action. Save promotions for a separate message.
- **Skipping consent:** Get permission at booking and honor every STOP reply.
