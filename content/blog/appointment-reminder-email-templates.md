> **Short answer:** A good appointment reminder email has a subject line with the date, the time and place, and a one-click way to confirm or reschedule. For example, subject "See you tomorrow at [Time]," then: "Hi [Name], just a reminder that your [Service] is tomorrow, [Date], at [Time]. Need to reschedule? [Link]." Send one a week before and one the day before.

Email is the best place for the details: directions, parking, forms and what to bring. A clear reminder email means fewer no-shows and fewer "where do I park?" calls.

This guide is for clinics, law firms, accounting offices and any business that books appointments. Below are 32 copy-and-paste reminder emails with subject lines, grouped by when and why you send them.

## What makes a good appointment reminder email?

- **Put the date in the subject line:** Clients should know what the email is about from their inbox.
- **Lead with the key facts:** Day, date, time and place go near the top, on their own lines.
- **Make the next step one click:** A confirm or reschedule link beats "call us during office hours."
- **Add prep notes only when needed:** Forms, documents or parking info help. Extra fluff gets skipped.
- **Use a real reply-to address:** Clients will hit reply. Make sure someone reads it.
- **Keep private details out:** Skip health conditions, case details and account numbers.

## When to send appointment reminder emails

| When | Email to send | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| Right after booking | Confirmation, or a welcome email for new clients | Include an add-to-calendar link |
| 1 week before | First reminder with a confirm link | Most useful for visits booked far ahead |
| 2 to 3 days before | Prep instructions or forms | Gives clients time to get ready |
| 1 day before | Day-before reminder | Add directions and parking |
| 1 hour before | Video link (virtual visits only) | Pair with a text for in-person visits |
| Same day, after a no-show | Gentle check-in with a rebook link | Keep the tone kind |

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Vague subject lines:** "Reminder" alone gets lost. Add the date or time.
- **Hiding the time:** Don't bury the appointment details under a long greeting.
- **No way to reschedule:** If the only option is calling, some people skip the visit instead.
- **Sending from a no-reply address:** Clients will reply with questions. Make sure those replies reach someone.
- **Too many emails:** Two reminders plus prep notes is usually enough.
- **Wrong time zone:** For video calls, state the time zone, especially if clients live in other states.
